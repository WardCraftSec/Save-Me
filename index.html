NextDNS is the service that does the filtering. It's free and takes about a minute.

Go to nextdns.io and tap Sign up.

Any email works — it doesn't have to be one you check often. You don't need to share your real name.

Step 2 of 4: Find your profile ID and hostname

Your profile ID is a string of characters (like a1b2c3d4). Your hostname is what you'll type into your phone or router later.

Once you're in the dashboard, look at the top. You'll see:

• Your profile ID (8 characters)
• Your hostname — it looks like a1b2c3d4.dns.nextdns.io

Keep both somewhere safe. You'll need the hostname in the next steps.

💡 The Setup tab on your dashboard also has quick instructions for each device type.

← Go back

  • Can't find the ID? It's at the very top of the dashboard, under your profile name.
  • It should look like 8 random characters. If you see a longer string, that might be your API key — the ID is shorter.

Step 3 of 4: Test your connection

One quick check to make sure your account is active.

Open test.nextdns.io in your browser.

You should see your profile ID displayed. That means the account is live and ready to use.

  • If the page loads but shows a different ID — you might be logged into a different account.

Step 1 of 4: Open your router (your home wifi) admin page

Every router (your home wifi) has a settings page. You access it from a phone or laptop that's connected to that wifi.

Open a browser and type: 192.168.0.1 or 192.168.1.1 (try both if one doesn't work).

It'll ask for a username and password. Check the sticker on the bottom or back of your router (your home wifi) — it's usually printed there.

← Go back

  • Neither address works? Check the sticker on your router (your home wifi) for the correct address.
  • Forgot the password? Long-press the reset button on the router for 10 seconds (this resets your wifi — you'll need to reconnect devices after).

Step 2 of 4: Find the DNS settings

DNS (the system that looks up where websites live) settings are usually under "Internet", "WAN", or "Network" in the router (your home wifi) menu.

Look for a field that says DNS server, Primary DNS, or DNS resolver (the service that answers those lookups).

There may be two fields (Primary and Secondary). You'll put your NextDNS hostname in the Primary one.

← Go back

  • Can't find it? Try looking under "Advanced settings" or "Internet settings" — routers (your home wifi) label things differently.
  • Some routers hide DNS behind "Custom DNS" or "Manual DNS".

Step 3 of 4: Enter your NextDNS hostname

This tells every device on your home wifi to look up websites through NextDNS instead of the default.

In the Primary DNS field, type your hostname from Step 2 of the "Get your DNS" tab. It looks like:

a1b2c3d4.dns.nextdns.io

Leave Secondary blank or put 1.1.1.1 (a backup). Tap Save.

⚠️ Important: This covers all devices using your home wifi at that time. Devices on mobile data, or using a different wifi (work, cafe, friend's home) won't go through this filter. To cover those, use the Other devices tab.

← Go back

  • Make sure there are no spaces before or after the hostname.
  • If the router (your home wifi) won't save — some routers only accept IP addresses, not hostnames. In that case, skip this tab and use the Other devices tab instead.

Step 4 of 4: Verify it's working

On any phone or laptop connected to your home wifi, open test.nextdns.io.

If it shows your profile ID → the router (your home wifi) is routing through NextDNS. Every device on this wifi is now filtered.

← Go back

  • Try forgetting your wifi and reconnecting (the device needs to pick up the new DNS).
  • Restart your router (your home wifi) — turn it off for 30 seconds, then back on.
  • Still not working? Your router (your home wifi) might not support custom DNS hostnames. Use the Other devices tab instead.

✅ Your home wifi is filtering.

Every device connected to this router (your home wifi) now goes through NextDNS.

Step 1 of 4: Why this tab exists

The router (your home wifi) filter only works on devices using that wifi. A phone on mobile data, at work, in a cafe, or at a friend's home won't go through it.

This tab sets up NextDNS directly on the device itself. It works on any network — mobile data, work wifi, cafe wifi, anywhere. The device always goes through NextDNS, no matter where it is.

Each device gets its own named profile in your NextDNS dashboard, with its own logs and its own controls.

← Go back

  • This doesn't replace the router (your home wifi) setup — it adds to it. A device can be covered by both.
  • You'll need to do this on each device separately.

Step 2 of 4: Create a profile for this device

Each device needs its own profile so you can see its logs separately.

In your NextDNS dashboard: tap + Create new profile (or "Add profile").

Give it a name you'll recognise — e.g. "Living room phone", "Bedside tablet".

It'll generate a new hostname for this profile. It'll look different from your first one (different 8 characters before .dns.nextdns.io).

← Go back

  • Can't find "Create new profile"? Look in the top-left menu or tap your current profile name to see a dropdown.
  • Make sure you're logged into the right NextDNS account.

Step 3 of 4: Set Private DNS on the device

This is the same setting as before, but you use the new hostname for this device's profile.

Android: Settings → Network & Internet → Private DNS → Enter provider hostname → type the new hostname → Save

iPhone: Go to nextdns.io → tap this profile → tap the iPhone setup link → Download Profile → Settings → Install

⚠️ This only works while the device has an internet connection (mobile data or wifi). If the device is in airplane mode, nothing gets logged.

← Go back

  • Make sure you're using the new hostname for this profile, not the original one.
  • No spaces in the hostname. No extra characters.
  • On iPhone: if "Profile Downloaded" doesn't appear in Settings, try the link again.

Step 4 of 4: Verify it appears in your dashboard

Go back to your NextDNS dashboard. You should now see two profiles (or more, if you've added others).

Tap the new one. You'll start seeing logs appear as the device uses the internet.

Each profile has its own Logs, its own Filters, its own Denylist. You can set different rules for different devices.

← Go back

  • Logs take a few minutes to appear. Use the device (open a website) and check back.
  • If the profile shows but no logs — the device might still be using the old DNS. Toggle Private DNS off and on again.

✅ That device is now tracked everywhere.

Mobile data, work wifi, cafe wifi — it all shows up in its own profile with its own logs.

Step 1 of 4: Turn on the Adult filter

NextDNS has a pre-made filter that blocks adult (porn) websites. It's one tap.

In your dashboard: tap Filters (left sidebar or top menu).

Under Privacy or Blocklists, find Adult and tick it.

Tap Save.

💡 There are other useful filters on this page too — AdGuard DNS Filter (ads & trackers), OISD (extra tracking). You can also block specific apps or add custom rules in other tabs on this page.

← Go back

  • Can't find "Adult"? It might be under "Privacy" sub-tab or listed as "Adult content" or "NSFW".
  • Make sure you're editing the right profile (if you have multiple devices, each has its own Filters page).

Step 2 of 4: Add specific websites to the Denylist

The Adult filter blocks categories. The Denylist blocks specific websites by name. Use it for sites you've already seen and want gone.

In your dashboard: Filters → Denylist (or "Blocklist" → "Custom").

Type the website (domain) you want blocked. E.g. example.com. Tap Add.

You can add as many as you need. Each one is blocked on every device using this profile.

← Go back

  • Type just the website name — no https://, no www unless it's specifically www.example.com you want to block.
  • One website per line if adding multiple.

Step 3 of 4: Read your logs — what the colours mean

Your logs show every website (domain) the device tried to visit, with a timestamp.

Each entry has a colour:

🟢 Green = allowed. You (or a filter) explicitly set this to be allowed.
🔴 Red = blocked. A filter or denylist rule stopped it.
⚪ White = allowed, but no rules have been applied to it yet. It's just getting through because nothing is blocking it.

What to do with white entries: If you see a website (domain) in white that you don't want there, copy it and add it to the Denylist (Step 2). It'll turn red next time the device tries to visit it.

← Go back

  • If everything is white — your filters might not be saving. Go back to Step 1 and confirm Adult is ticked and Saved.
  • Logs only show entries from after you set up the filter. Older entries won't change colour.

Step 4 of 4: Ongoing — check logs regularly

Filters block categories, but new websites appear all the time. The logs are how you catch the ones that slip through.

Every so often, open Logs and scan for white entries you don't recognise. If one looks unwanted:

1. Tap it to copy the website (domain)
2. Go to the Site Checker tab to look into it
3. Add it to the Denylist

This takes about 2 minutes. You don't need to do it every day — a few times a week is enough.

← Go back

  • You don't need to act on every white entry. Most are normal background traffic (app updates, analytics, etc.).
  • Only add to the Denylist what you specifically don't want.

✅ You're set up.

The filters run in the background. You don't need to do anything except check the logs now and then.
